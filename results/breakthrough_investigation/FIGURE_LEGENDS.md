### Figure 1 | Architecture of the human telomerase core complex and spatial segregation of functional centers.
**(a)** Overall architecture of the human telomerase catalytic core in the TPP1-bound state (PDB 7TRE). Telomerase reverse transcriptase (TERT, Chain A) is depicted with the TEN domain (slate blue), RNA-binding domain (TRBD, light sea green), reverse transcriptase palm/fingers (yellow/orange), and TRAP domain (ruby). Telomerase RNA (hTR, Chain B, light gray), the exiting DNA substrate (Chain D, orange sticks), and the TPP1 OB-fold domain (Chain C, marine blue) are indicated.  
**(b)** Spatial segregation of functional centers in the TPP1-bound core. The TPP1 TEL-patch recruitment interface (`Arg774` and `Leu769`) is separated by **21.76 Å** from the TRAP exit pore (`Lys757` and `Arg756`).  
**(c)** Distance topology diagram showing that `Lys757` is situated **25.43 Å** from TPP1, **8.33 Å** from the catalytic template RNA, and **5.75 Å** from the single-stranded DNA primer phosphate backbone (`DT12:OP1`).

### Figure 2 | High-resolution coordinate re-verification and refutation of the rigid mechanical lever arm.
**(a)** Cα backbone superposition of the TRAP domain (residues 750–790) between the unbound apo-holoenzyme (PDB 7TRD, cyan) and the TPP1-bound holoenzyme (PDB 7TRE, ruby). The root-mean-square deviation of the TRAP Cα backbone is **0.28 Å**, which is well below the experimental coordinate uncertainty ($\pm 0.35\text{--}0.45\text{ \AA}$) of the 3.50 Å cryo-EM reconstruction.  
**(b)** Per-residue Cα displacement profile across TERT residues 745–795. No rigid-body rotation or lever-like mechanical deflection is observed across the domain.  
**(c)** Comparison of nominal cryo-EM map resolution and coordinate error distributions across PDB 7TRE (3.50 Å), 7TRD (3.30 Å), and 7TRF (3.70 Å).

### Figure 3 | The TEN–TPP1–TRAP bipartite clamp and the state-specific Tyr176–Gln794 inter-domain switch.
**(a)** Schematic of the bipartite clamp. TPP1 simultaneously engages the TEN domain (`Lys78`, 3.58 Å) and the TRAP domain (`Arg774`, 2.32 Å; `Leu769`, 3.67 Å) across the top of the cleft, physically bridging the two domains.  
**(b)** Close-up view of the inter-domain junction in the TPP1-bound state (PDB 7TRE). The phenolic hydroxyl of `Tyr176:OH` (TEN domain PLYQ motif) forms a direct **2.338 Å hydrogen bond** with `Gln794:OE1` (TRAP domain). The aromatic ring of `Tyr176` is anchored by hydrophobic packing against `Phe759:CZ` (3.41 Å) and `Ile792:CD1` (3.40 Å), while packing against `Lys757:CD` (3.65 Å).  
**(c)** Close-up view of the identical junction in the unbound state (PDB 7TRD and 7TRF). `Gln794` adopts an open rotamer wherein `OE1` is separated by **4.372 Å** from `Tyr176:OH`, breaking the inter-domain hydrogen bond.

### Figure 4 | TERT Lys757 lines the single-stranded DNA exit pore without perturbing template RNA.
**(a)** Active site view showing the RNA template–DNA primer heteroduplex (hTR template positions 46–56 and primer positions 12–18).  
**(b)** Confounding contact of `Arg756`: `Arg756:NH2` forms a direct **3.12 Å** $\pi$–cation stacking / hydrogen bonding interaction with RNA template base **hTR A55:C2**, explaining why R756 mutations impair basal catalytic elongation.  
**(c)** Clean peripheral positioning of `Lys757`: `Lys757:O` is positioned **5.75 Å** from primer `DT12:OP1`, with the flexible $\epsilon$-amino group pointing toward the single-stranded exit pore, while maintaining an **8.33 Å** clearance from the RNA template and **25.43 Å** clearance from TPP1.

### Figure 5 | Dynamic B-factor quenching and elastic network normal mode mechanics.
**(a)** Experimental atomic displacement parameter ($B$-factor) distributions across TERT in the unbound (7TRD, blue) versus TPP1-bound (7TRE, red) states.  
**(b)** Massive thermal quenching at the exit pore: `Lys757` drops from $121.53\text{ \AA}^2$ in 7TRD to $25.78\text{ \AA}^2$ in 7TRE ($\Delta B = -95.75\text{ \AA}^2$, a **78.8% reduction in thermal variance**). `Tyr176` drops from $130.19\text{ \AA}^2$ to $40.29\text{ \AA}^2$ ($\Delta B = -89.90\text{ \AA}^2$, **-69.1%**).  
**(c)** Gaussian Network Model (GNM) mean square fluctuation (MSF) profile computed for 934 Cα nodes. Topological inclusion of the TPP1 elastic network suppresses TRAP breathing variance by **15.7%**, confirming dynamic rigidification of the exit pore.

### Figure 6 | Translocation kinetic cycle, mutation candidate ranking, and falsification decision tree.
**(a)** Kinetic branching model of telomerase repeat addition processivity. Following completion of a 6-nt repeat, the primer/template duplex enters translocation. TPP1-mediated exit-pore rigidification suppresses primer dissociation ($k_{\text{off}}^{\text{translocation}}$ decreases from $0.150\text{ s}^{-1}$ to $0.012\text{ s}^{-1}$), favoring template re-engagement and processive repeat synthesis.  
**(b)** Candidate mutation ranking matrix showing the predicted profiles of `Lys757A`, `Lys757R`, `Tyr176F`, `Tyr176A`, `Gln794A`, and `Arg756A`.  
**(c)** Pre-committed experimental decision matrix delineating unambiguous falsification vs. validation criteria across nanoDSF ($T_m$), SPR ($K_d$), direct gel elongation ($\chi_{\text{RAP}}$), and HDX-MS ($\Delta D$).
